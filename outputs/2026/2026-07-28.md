## 1. Uribe's conviction becomes a reversible rule-of-law stress test

**Original date:** 2025-07-28
**Type:** Courts / Democratic Accountability / Colombia
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On July 28, 2025, a Colombian judge convicted former President Alvaro Uribe of abuse of process and bribery of a public official in a long-running witness-tampering case. Reuters reported that the verdict made him Colombia's first former president to be found guilty at trial and came less than a year before the 2026 presidential election.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the July verdict stands as a historically important trial judgment but not as a settled conviction. Uribe was sentenced in August 2025, then released while appealing, and in October 2025 a Bogota appeals court struck down the convictions after finding the trial evidence legally insufficient. The case remained politically live because a further appeal could move it toward Colombia's Supreme Court.

### Direct impacts
The immediate impact was to place one of Colombia's most influential modern politicians under a criminal judgment and sentencing process. It also gave victims' representatives and Uribe's opponents a formal trial finding after years of litigation, while giving Uribe's defense a clear appellate target.

### Indirect impacts
The case became a test of judicial independence, evidentiary standards, and political polarization around Colombia's armed-conflict legacy. It also entered the 2026 electoral environment, where Uribe's party and allies remained active even as the legal record shifted.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage could capture the unprecedented conviction but not its durability. The one-year view shows that the most important institutional fact was the appellate filter: a landmark trial judgment could still be reversed without ending the broader dispute over accountability and political legitimacy.

### One-year assessment
The July 28 verdict mattered because it showed that a former Colombian president could be convicted at trial, but its later reversal prevents any simple accountability narrative. Its lasting value is as a record of how high-profile corruption and witness-tampering allegations move through a polarized but functioning appellate system.

## 2. New Zealand's voting-law warning becomes election-year statute

**Original date:** 2025-07-28
**Type:** Electoral Law / Voting Rights / New Zealand
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On July 28, 2025, New Zealand Attorney-General Judith Collins warned that the government's electoral reform package could be inconsistent with rights protections. The contested measures included closing voter enrolment 13 days before election day, reinstating a wider prisoner voting disqualification, and restricting free food, drink, or entertainment near polling places.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the warning had become part of the record behind enacted law. Parliament passed the Electoral Amendment Act 2025 in December, and the Ministry of Justice says most provisions came into force on December 20, 2025. The changes now shape electoral administration ahead of the 2026 general election.

### Direct impacts
The law moved the enrolment cutoff to 13 days before polling day, set a 12-day advance voting period, changed electoral-roll administration, reinstated prisoner voting restrictions for shorter sentences, and created a new offence around providing free food, drink, or entertainment within 100 metres of voting places.

### Indirect impacts
The episode exposed a tradeoff between faster official counts and access to the franchise. It also showed the limits of attorney-general rights reporting in New Zealand's parliamentary system: a rights-warning can sharpen public scrutiny without stopping a government bill from passing.

### What original coverage missed
The immediate controversy focused on whether the proposals were justified. The later record shows the more durable result: the changes survived the legislative process and became operational rules for an election cycle rather than remaining a short-lived political argument.

### One-year assessment
The July 28 warning mattered because it marked the point at which technical electoral-administration changes became a rights issue. One year later, the archival significance is clear: New Zealand chose administrative speed and integrity measures while accepting a formally recorded rights concern about voter access.

## 3. The Tesla-Samsung chip deal becomes a Texas foundry timeline test

**Original date:** 2025-07-28
**Type:** Semiconductors / Industrial Policy / AI Hardware
**Confidence:** Medium

### Then
On July 28, 2025, Tesla and Samsung's semiconductor supply agreement became public after Elon Musk said Samsung's Taylor, Texas fab would make Tesla's next-generation AI6 chip. Reuters reported that the deal was worth $16.5 billion and could support Samsung's struggling contract-manufacturing business, while noting that production was years away.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the deal had not yet produced volume chips, but it had become a concrete production-timeline test for Samsung's U.S. foundry ambitions. Reuters reported in March 2026 that a Samsung executive expected Tesla chips based on Samsung's advanced 2-nanometer process in the second half of 2027. Korean reporting in July 2026 said Samsung had completed its version of Tesla's AI5 chip design and was preparing Taylor production, while the larger roadmap still depended on ramping an advanced process at a delayed facility.

### Direct impacts
The deal gave Samsung a high-profile foundry customer, strengthened the business case for the Taylor fab, and tied Tesla's self-driving, robotics, and AI infrastructure ambitions to a U.S.-based Samsung production line. It also gave investors a visible milestone for measuring Samsung's ability to compete beyond memory chips.

### Indirect impacts
The agreement linked industrial policy, supply-chain localization, and AI hardware strategy. It made Tesla less dependent on a single foundry path only if Samsung could hit process and yield targets, and it made Samsung's Texas project part of the broader competition over advanced-node manufacturing outside Taiwan.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage correctly stressed strategic value but could not know whether the Taylor fab would become a near-term manufacturing asset or a delayed option. The one-year record shows the latter nuance: the project is active and more specific, but still mostly measured by tape-out claims, pilot work, and 2027 expectations.

### One-year assessment
The July 28 announcement mattered less as an immediate Tesla product event than as a long-horizon supply-chain commitment. One year later, its significance is still conditional: it strengthened Samsung's U.S. foundry story, but the real test remains whether advanced-node production reaches usable volume on schedule.

## 4. The adult-games purge turns payment rails into platform governance

**Original date:** 2025-07-28
**Type:** Digital Governance / Payments / Platform Moderation
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On July 28, 2025, itch.io added a public FAQ to its earlier announcement that it had deindexed adult NSFW content from browse and search. The platform said scrutiny from payment processors, following a campaign against Steam and itch.io, forced urgent compliance steps to protect payment infrastructure for all creators.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the episode had settled into a narrower but still consequential platform-governance precedent. itch.io reindexed free adult NSFW content on July 31, 2025, while saying paid adult content would return more slowly and that Stripe payments for adult NSFW pages had been disabled. The broader controversy continued to be cited in later disputes over Steam review and adult-content enforcement.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was loss of discovery for many adult games and uncertainty for creators who depended on marketplace search and platform-managed payments. Some content remained accessible to owners, but deindexing reduced visibility and shifted the practical burden onto creators to fit payment-processor rules.

### Indirect impacts
The episode showed that payment processors can function as private regulators of digital expression even when storefronts and users are not asking for a legal takedown. It especially affected smaller platforms, which have less negotiating power and may respond with broader restrictions than larger, more curated marketplaces.

### What original coverage missed
Early coverage emphasized censorship backlash and the number of affected games. The one-year lesson is more structural: payment dependency can make moderation policy less a matter of storefront preference than of banking access, risk tolerance, and merchant-of-record obligations.

### One-year assessment
The July 28 FAQ mattered because it made the governance mechanism explicit. It did not end adult content on itch.io, but it documented how financial infrastructure can shape what lawful material is searchable, sellable, and sustainable on open digital platforms.
