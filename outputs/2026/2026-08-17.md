## 1. Bolivia's first round becomes a post-MAS transition vote

**Original date:** 2025-08-17
**Type:** Democratic Transition / Electoral Politics / Economic Crisis
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-08-17, Bolivians voted in a presidential election shaped by inflation, fuel shortages, dollar scarcity, and the fragmentation of the Movement for Socialism. Early results showed centrist Rodrigo Paz unexpectedly ahead and conservative former president Jorge Quiroga in second place, sending the country toward an October runoff and leaving MAS with its weakest national showing in a generation.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the first round reads as the opening break in Bolivia's long MAS era. The electoral tribunal later confirmed Paz's runoff victory with 54.96% of valid votes, and his government entered office without an easy legislative majority while facing the same economic crisis that had driven the electoral shift. By mid-2026, Bolivia had ended its 15-year dollar peg and reached a staff-level IMF agreement intended to rebuild reserves and ease the dollar shortage.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was a confirmed transfer path away from MAS rule. Paz and Quiroga displaced the old ruling-party field, the August vote forced a runoff, and the October result installed a new president whose mandate was tied directly to economic stabilization rather than ideological continuity.

### Indirect impacts
The vote converted Bolivia's fiscal, fuel, and foreign-exchange stress into a governing test. The exchange-rate move, IMF talks, and protest pressure that followed show that the election did not resolve the crisis; it changed who would bear responsibility for managing it and how much social tolerance existed for adjustment.

### What original coverage missed
Immediate coverage captured the surprise first-round result and the scale of the MAS defeat. It could not yet show that Paz would win the runoff, that his administration would abandon the long-standing dollar peg, or that the new government would quickly confront blockade politics and external-financing negotiations.

### One-year assessment
Bolivia's first round became a post-MAS transition vote. Its significance lies less in the single-day result than in the way a fragmented electorate used an economic crisis to end one dominant political cycle while handing the successor government a narrow, unstable stabilization mandate.

## 2. Kuwait's methanol arrests become a migrant-health warning

**Original date:** 2025-08-17
**Type:** Public Health / Migrant Workers / Illicit Markets
**Confidence:** Medium

### Then
On 2025-08-17, Kuwaiti authorities said they had arrested 67 people accused of producing and distributing locally made alcohol linked to a methanol-poisoning outbreak. Health officials had recorded 160 poisoning cases and 23 deaths, mostly among Asian nationals, with many patients needing ventilation or emergency dialysis.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the case stands as a concentrated record of how an illicit market can become a public-health disaster for migrant communities. Later local reporting described severe outcomes including brain-death cases and organ donation from some victims, while public evidence of a broader policy correction remained limited. The durable record is therefore medical, enforcement-based, and social rather than a completed reform story.

### Direct impacts
The direct impacts were deaths, intensive-care admissions, kidney dialysis, vision loss, arrests, and the seizure of illegal production sites. Embassies for affected workers became involved in tracing and assisting citizens, and hospitals had to manage a sudden cluster of toxic-alcohol cases.

### Indirect impacts
The outbreak exposed the tension between prohibition, underground supply, and low-wage migrant exposure. It also showed how enforcement narratives can focus on traffickers while the most visible burden falls on foreign workers who have limited public voice in the host country's policy debate.

### What original coverage missed
The first reports could count arrests and fatalities, but they could not yet show the medical aftermath for patients who survived with organ damage, the organ-donation consequence, or whether the crackdown would translate into durable harm-reduction or labor-protection changes.

### One-year assessment
Kuwait's methanol arrests became a migrant-health warning. The event did not become a widely traceable reform milestone, but it left a clear archival lesson: when demand is pushed into an unregulated market, product safety, migrant vulnerability, and criminal enforcement can converge in a lethal way.

## 3. Hurricane Erin becomes an offshore coastal-risk rehearsal

**Original date:** 2025-08-17
**Type:** Disaster Preparedness / Coastal Infrastructure / Hurricane Risk
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-08-17, Hurricane Erin was a dangerous major hurricane north of Puerto Rico and east of the Turks and Caicos, with the National Hurricane Center warning that it could strengthen again and that interests in the Bahamas, Bermuda, and the North Carolina Outer Banks should monitor the storm. Its core was forecast to stay offshore, but its size and surf hazards were already central to the risk picture.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, Erin reads as a near-miss that still required real coastal management. NOAA's 2025 Atlantic summary lists Erin as a major hurricane with maximum winds of 140 knots, a minimum pressure of 913 mb, 13 direct deaths, and limited U.S. property damage. North Carolina officials later documented Outer Banks flooding, NC 12 closures, dune breaches, evacuations, ferry disruptions, and business interruption even though the hurricane did not make a U.S. landfall.

### Direct impacts
The direct impacts included tropical-storm warnings, dangerous surf and rip currents, rain bands in the Caribbean, evacuation orders for exposed Outer Banks areas, road and ferry closures, overwash, dune damage, and localized business disruption. The storm also forced emergency managers to communicate that distance from the eye did not mean absence of danger.

### Indirect impacts
Erin reinforced the importance of surge, surf, road-continuity, and barrier-island planning for large offshore hurricanes. It also showed the value and limits of forecast lead time: officials could avoid a landfall disaster, but still had to manage travel, evacuation, shoreline access, and repeated high-tide hazards.

### What original coverage missed
The August 17 advisories could describe the forecast threat, but they could not yet show how large Erin would become, how far its impacts would reach, or the specific Outer Banks infrastructure effects that followed several days later.

### One-year assessment
Hurricane Erin became an offshore coastal-risk rehearsal. Its consequence was not a catastrophic landfall, but a well-documented reminder that a large hurricane can remain offshore and still impose life-safety, transportation, and coastal-infrastructure costs across a long shoreline.

## 4. Advent's u-blox offer becomes a GNSS take-private

**Original date:** 2025-08-17
**Type:** Semiconductors / Industrial Technology / Private Equity
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-08-17, u-blox announced that Advent International, through ZI Zenith, would launch a public tender offer for all publicly held u-blox shares at CHF 135 per share. The offer valued the Swiss positioning and short-range communications company at about CHF 1.05 billion and had the unanimous recommendation of u-blox's board.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the announcement had become a completed take-private transaction. Advent completed the acquisition in November 2025 after regulatory approvals and satisfaction of offer conditions, held more than 98% of u-blox shares, and moved toward squeeze-out and delisting. u-blox also shifted leadership, with Andreas Thiel and Camila Japur set to become co-CEOs from January 2026.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was the removal of a listed Swiss GNSS and wireless-connectivity supplier from ordinary public-market ownership. Shareholders who tendered received CHF 135 per share, Advent gained control, and the company entered a private-equity ownership model aimed at growth in automotive, industrial, and adjacent markets.

### Indirect impacts
The deal placed a specialized European positioning-chip and connectivity supplier inside a broader private-equity industrial-technology portfolio. That matters because precise positioning, embedded intelligence, and secure connectivity are enabling layers for vehicles, industrial equipment, logistics, and IoT systems, even when they attract less attention than leading-edge AI chips.

### What original coverage missed
The initial offer coverage could identify the price, premium, and board support. It could not yet show that the offer would close, that Advent would cross the 98% threshold, or that delisting and a leadership transition would follow quickly.

### One-year assessment
Advent's u-blox offer became a GNSS take-private. Its consequence was not a broad semiconductor shock, but a meaningful ownership shift for a niche technology supplier whose products sit inside larger automotive, industrial, and location-dependent systems.
