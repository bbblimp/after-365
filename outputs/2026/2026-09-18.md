## 1. The ACIP meeting becomes a vaccine-guidance implementation test

**Original date:** 2025-09-18
**Type:** Public Health / Vaccine Policy / Scientific Governance
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-09-18, the CDC's Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices opened a two-day meeting that took up measles-mumps-rubella-varicella vaccination, the hepatitis B birth dose, and COVID-19 vaccination. The meeting came after unusual turbulence in U.S. vaccine governance, including changes in CDC leadership and the composition of the advisory panel.

### State after 365 days
A year later, the meeting's most consequential practical result was visible in official COVID-19 vaccination guidance. HHS announced on 2025-09-19 that ACIP recommended COVID-19 vaccination for people six months and older through individual decision-making, and CDC's 2025-2026 guidance later reflected that approach. By September 2026, reporting on delayed public vaccine program ordering and measles resurgence showed how guidance changes were interacting with access and confidence problems.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was to move COVID-19 vaccination away from a broad default recommendation and into an individual or shared clinical decision-making framework. CDC guidance also had to explain how that framework applied across age, vaccination history, and risk categories.

### Indirect impacts
The broader effect was to make vaccine access depend more heavily on interpretation by clinicians, parents, insurers, pharmacies, and public programs. That increased the administrative importance of schedules, coverage rules, and communication at a time when confidence in vaccination was already politically contested.

### What original coverage missed
First-day coverage could identify the topics before the committee and the political controversy around the panel, but it could not yet show the operational guidance that would follow or the later access problems in fall 2026 vaccination programs.

### One-year assessment
The September 18 meeting now reads as a governance turning point rather than a narrow advisory calendar item. Its significance lies less in one vote than in the way scientific recommendation, political oversight, and practical vaccine access became entangled in the year that followed.

## 2. New Mexico's universal child-care promise becomes a capacity test

**Original date:** 2025-09-18
**Type:** Social Policy / Child Care / State Governance
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-09-18, national coverage highlighted New Mexico's plan to become the first U.S. state to offer free child care regardless of family income. The program was scheduled to begin on 2025-11-01, remove income eligibility limits and copayments, and rely heavily on the state's oil-and-gas-backed early childhood funds.

### State after 365 days
A year later, the policy had moved from announcement to law and operation. Governor Michelle Lujan Grisham signed universal child care legislation in March 2026, and state materials said the program launched on 2025-11-01, added thousands of families and children, and supported new child-care capacity. Follow-up reporting in September 2026, however, found that demand was still outpacing available slots in some communities and that costs were rising.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was to remove income limits and copays from New Mexico's child-care assistance program for eligible working or studying families. The state also committed large appropriations and provider-support measures intended to increase supply.

### Indirect impacts
The indirect effect was to turn New Mexico into a national test case for whether a state can finance broad child-care entitlement-like access without first solving workforce, facility, infant-care, and rural supply constraints.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage captured the ambition and fiscal base of the program, but it could not yet show how quickly families would enroll, how many providers would expand, or how strongly shortages would persist after tuition barriers were removed.

### One-year assessment
The announcement was consequential because it changed the child-care policy frontier from targeted subsidy to universal access. After one year, the record is mixed but important: New Mexico proved the financing and legal model could be built, while also showing that affordability reforms do not automatically create enough care.

## 3. The Ticketmaster case becomes part of a wider gatekeeper reckoning

**Original date:** 2025-09-18
**Type:** Consumer Protection / Antitrust / Live Entertainment
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-09-18, the Federal Trade Commission and seven states sued Live Nation and Ticketmaster, alleging deceptive ticket pricing, weakly enforced purchase limits, and coordination that let brokers obtain tickets beyond stated caps and resell them at higher prices. The case framed ticketing not only as an antitrust problem, but also as a consumer-protection and BOTS Act enforcement issue.

### State after 365 days
A year later, the FTC case was still pending, with the FTC case page listing a March 2026 opposition to the defendants' motion to dismiss. At the same time, the separate Justice Department and state antitrust case against Live Nation and Ticketmaster had moved much further: DOJ posted a proposed final judgment in June 2026, while state plaintiffs continued litigating remedies after a jury verdict. Live Nation's 2026 securities filing treated the FTC case and the antitrust case as continuing legal risks.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was a federal and state enforcement action seeking injunctions, civil penalties, and monetary relief over alleged hidden-fee and broker-limit practices. It gave regulators a separate consumer-protection path alongside the larger monopoly case.

### Indirect impacts
The case increased pressure on Ticketmaster's pricing disclosures, resale-market practices, and relationship with brokers. It also made live-event ticketing part of a broader regulatory campaign against digital gatekeepers whose rules may shape both market access and consumer prices.

### What original coverage missed
The first-day complaint could not show how the case would interact with the older DOJ antitrust action. One year later, that interaction is clearer: the FTC suit remained pending while the antitrust case produced proposed structural and conduct remedies.

### One-year assessment
The September 18 suit has not yet produced final relief, but it has become consequential as one layer in a broader legal squeeze on Live Nation and Ticketmaster. Its importance is in preserving a consumer-deception theory even as antitrust remedies proceed on a separate track.

## 4. The Canada-Mexico partnership becomes a diversification marker

**Original date:** 2025-09-18
**Type:** Trade Diplomacy / North American Relations / Economic Security
**Confidence:** Medium

### Then
On 2025-09-18, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney met Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum in Mexico City. The leaders announced a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership and a 2025-2028 action plan covering trade, infrastructure, energy, agriculture, health, border security, organized crime, emergency preparedness, climate action, and coordination ahead of the Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement review.

### State after 365 days
A year later, the Mexico visit looked like an early part of a wider Canadian diversification strategy. In September 2026, Carney welcomed a European proposal to make Canada the EU's first associate member and argued for deeper integration with Europe across trade, defense, critical minerals, AI, energy, space, research, and financial services. AP reporting described the push as a response to strained U.S. ties and economic pressure.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was to elevate Canada-Mexico relations from periodic trilateral coordination to a named bilateral strategic partnership with a three-year action plan and planned ministerial follow-up.

### Indirect impacts
The indirect effect was to signal that Canada and Mexico were preparing for North American trade uncertainty by strengthening their own channel. It also gave Canada a diplomatic template for the broader middle-power diversification message Carney emphasized in 2026.

### What original coverage missed
Immediate coverage could identify the partnership and USMCA review context, but it could not yet show how quickly the same logic would appear in Canada's outreach to Europe under pressure from Washington.

### One-year assessment
The September 18 partnership was not a dramatic break with the United States, but it now reads as part of a larger hedging pattern. Its consequence lies in showing two U.S. neighbors trying to build more bilateral resilience before North American trade rules came under renewed strain.
