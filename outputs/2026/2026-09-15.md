## 1. The WTO fisheries-subsidies pact becomes an implementation test

**Original date:** 2025-09-15
**Type:** Trade Governance / Ocean Sustainability / Multilateral Rules
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-09-15, the WTO Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies entered into force after the two-thirds acceptance threshold was reached. WTO material described it as the organization's first multilateral agreement with environmental sustainability at its core, prohibiting subsidies tied to illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing, overfished stocks, and unregulated high-seas fishing.

### State after 365 days
A year later, the agreement had shifted from ratification milestone to implementation mechanics. WTO records show the first Fisheries Subsidies Committee meeting adopted procedural rules in May 2026, while the Fish Fund had approved support for more than 40 developing and least-developed members by July 2026. Negotiations on additional subsidy disciplines also remained open after MC14.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was to insert new fisheries-subsidy disciplines into the WTO legal framework for accepting members. It also activated oversight, notification, and technical-assistance structures that had been waiting for entry into force.

### Indirect impacts
The pact gave the WTO a rare recent example of binding multilateral rule-making in an environmental domain. For coastal and developing economies, its practical value depends less on the ceremony of entry into force than on data, enforcement capacity, subsidy transparency, and access to Fish Fund assistance.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage properly emphasized the diplomatic breakthrough after more than two decades of negotiation. It could not yet show whether the agreement would produce working committee procedures, funded implementation projects, or further disciplines on the subsidies not fully covered by the first text.

### One-year assessment
The September 15 entry into force now looks consequential but incomplete. It created a real legal and institutional floor for fisheries-subsidy reform, while leaving the harder test in national implementation, disclosure, and follow-on negotiations.

## 2. The CHP court delay becomes an opposition-leadership rupture

**Original date:** 2025-09-15
**Type:** Democratic Institutions / Opposition Politics / Judicial Politics
**Confidence:** Medium

### Then
On 2025-09-15, an Ankara court delayed until October a case seeking to annul the 2023 congress that had made Ozgur Ozel leader of Turkey's main opposition Republican People's Party. Contemporary reporting noted that plaintiffs sought the return of former leader Kemal Kilicdaroglu and that the CHP had tried to protect its leadership through an extraordinary convention.

### State after 365 days
The delayed case did not remain procedural background. In May 2026, an Ankara appeals court ordered Ozel and the CHP leadership removed and restored Kilicdaroglu's pre-2023 team on an interim basis, according to Human Rights Watch and Turkish reporting. By July 2026, Turkish reporting said the appeal had moved to the Court of Cassation, leaving the leadership dispute unresolved at the national high-court level.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact of the September hearing was to keep the annulment threat alive while denying immediate interim removal. That mattered because the same legal track later became the vehicle for a much more intrusive court intervention in the main opposition party.

### Indirect impacts
The case deepened the overlap between internal party procedure, courts, and Turkey's broader contest over opposition space. It came after pressure on CHP mayors and officials, and it made party leadership itself a legal vulnerability rather than only an internal political question.

### What original coverage missed
Coverage on the day could see that the case was politically sensitive, but not that the delay would become a prelude to a 2026 appellate ruling removing the sitting party leadership. It also could not yet measure how far the dispute would travel through appeals.

### One-year assessment
The September 15 delay now reads as a hinge point in a judicialized opposition crisis. Its consequence was not the delay itself, but the preservation of a legal path that later disrupted the leadership of Turkey's principal opposition party.

## 3. The Philippine flood-control probe becomes a high-level corruption case

**Original date:** 2025-09-15
**Type:** Anti-Corruption / Infrastructure Governance / Disaster Resilience
**Confidence:** Medium

### Then
On 2025-09-15, President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. said the newly created Independent Commission for Infrastructure would meet daily and move quickly on alleged flood-control project anomalies. Philippine official releases said former Supreme Court justice Andres Reyes would chair the commission, with a mandate to examine infrastructure irregularities and propose system changes.

### State after 365 days
A year later, the issue had moved beyond a promise of review. Philippine News Agency reported in October 2025 that the commission recommended charges against senators, a former party-list lawmaker, public works officials, and others. By 2026, Philippine outlets and AP reporting described arrests, court cases, recovered funds, and a plunder case involving former House Speaker Martin Romualdez, a Marcos cousin who denied wrongdoing.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was the creation and activation of an independent investigative channel outside ordinary congressional hearings. It also froze or redirected parts of the 2026 flood-control budget and placed contractor, public works, and legislative practices under formal scrutiny.

### Indirect impacts
The scandal linked corruption control to disaster resilience in a country repeatedly exposed to typhoons and flooding. It also tested whether an administration led by a member of a powerful political family would pursue cases touching allies and relatives.

### What original coverage missed
The first-day framing focused on the commission's composition and speed. It could not yet show whether the probe would stop at public messaging or reach indictments, arrests, asset recovery, and allegations against senior political figures.

### One-year assessment
The September 15 commission launch now looks like an important institutional trigger. The ultimate legal outcomes remain unresolved, but the probe clearly moved from administrative review into a major corruption-enforcement sequence.

## 4. Trump's New York Times lawsuit becomes a press-litigation pressure point

**Original date:** 2025-09-15
**Type:** Media Law / Press Freedom / Civil Litigation
**Confidence:** Medium

### Then
On 2025-09-15, President Donald Trump filed a $15 billion defamation lawsuit in Florida federal court against The New York Times, Penguin Random House, and individual journalists over a book and articles about his business record. Reuters described it as part of a broader legal assault on major media companies he accused of unfair treatment.

### State after 365 days
The case did not immediately become a merits ruling. A federal judge struck the original complaint within days as procedurally improper and too long, then allowed refiling. Case-tracking records show an amended complaint was filed in October 2025, and 2026 reporting said motions to dismiss and further amendment practice kept the case alive rather than resolved.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was to force a major newspaper, publisher, and journalists into active federal litigation with the sitting president. It also created an early judicial rebuke over pleading form, narrowing the case into a more conventional litigation posture.

### Indirect impacts
The lawsuit became part of a larger debate over whether very large defamation claims by powerful officials chill investigative reporting even when courts later narrow or reject claims. Its importance lies as much in pressure and process as in any eventual damages outcome.

### What original coverage missed
The filing-day story captured the size of the demand and the media-politics context. It could not yet know that the first complaint would be struck almost immediately, or that the dispute would persist in amended form into 2026.

### One-year assessment
The lawsuit's first year shows limited legal resolution but meaningful institutional friction. It stands as a marker of how media disputes under Trump moved from rhetoric and settlement pressure into repeated federal-court contests.
