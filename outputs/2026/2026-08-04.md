## 1. Boeing Defense's St. Louis strike becomes a defense-production labor test

**Original date:** 2025-08-04
**Type:** Labor / Defense Manufacturing / Industrial Capacity
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-08-04, more than 3,200 International Association of Machinists and Aerospace Workers members at Boeing Defense facilities in the St. Louis area and Illinois went on strike after rejecting a second contract offer. The workers assembled F-15 and F/A-18 fighters, T-7 trainers, munitions, and 777X wing sections.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the strike reads as more than a local contract dispute. It lasted 101 days, involved repeated rejected offers, Boeing's threat to hire permanent replacement workers, and ended in November 2025 with a five-year contract approved by 68 percent of voting members. Reuters reported that the walkout disrupted F-15 deliveries and slowed several key programs.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was a prolonged shutdown by a skilled defense-production workforce at a time when Boeing was expanding St. Louis-area capacity for major military-aircraft programs. Boeing used non-union personnel to continue some work, but acknowledged slowed output during the dispute.

### Indirect impacts
The broader effect was to connect aerospace labor bargaining to defense-industrial resilience. The strike showed that military production capacity depends not only on contracts, plants, and procurement awards, but also on retention, wage progression, retirement terms, and relations with a specialized union workforce.

### What original coverage missed
Opening coverage captured the walkout and rejected offer, but it could not yet show the 101-day duration, the replacement-worker escalation, the several failed ratification attempts, or the eventual terms that returned workers to production in mid-November.

### One-year assessment
The strike was consequential because it turned a contract vote into a live test of whether defense manufacturing can absorb prolonged labor disruption without undermining delivery schedules. Its one-year meaning is not that labor won every demand, but that skilled aerospace labor became a visible constraint inside a strategic production base.

## 2. Milei's vetoes become a fiscal-surplus limit test

**Original date:** 2025-08-04
**Type:** Fiscal Policy / Social Protection / Legislative Checks
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-08-04, Argentine President Javier Milei vetoed laws that would have raised pension payments and strengthened protections and funding for people with disabilities. His government argued that Congress had approved spending without financing and that the measures threatened the fiscal balance central to his austerity program.

### State after 365 days
One year later, the vetoes stand as an early marker of the limits of executive austerity when it met organized legislative resistance. The pension increase was upheld after the opposition failed to reach the required two-thirds threshold in the lower house, but Congress overrode the disability-law veto in September 2025. The government then enacted the disability emergency law while trying to suspend implementation, before a February 2026 regulation set out implementing rules after judicial pressure.

### Direct impacts
The immediate impact was to block, at least temporarily, pension and disability measures approved by Congress and to force lawmakers to test whether they could assemble veto-proof majorities. The disability measure survived that test; the pension increase did not.

### Indirect impacts
The wider effect was to make disability policy a constitutional and fiscal confrontation rather than a routine spending dispute. The episode exposed the tension between macroeconomic stabilization through spending restraint and social-protection claims by groups that had already absorbed sharp adjustment costs.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage could report the vetoes and the stated fiscal rationale, but it could not yet show the split outcome: a sustained presidential victory on general pension increases, a congressional override on disability funding, and later litigation and regulation over how the disability law would actually operate.

### One-year assessment
The vetoes were consequential because they mapped the practical boundary of Milei's fiscal-surplus politics. Austerity remained strong enough to defeat a broad pension increase, but not strong enough to prevent disability protections from becoming law after congressional and judicial pressure.

## 3. The plastics-health warning becomes a treaty-deadlock marker

**Original date:** 2025-08-04
**Type:** Environmental Health / Plastics / Multilateral Governance
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-08-04, researchers announced The Lancet Countdown on Health and Plastics as governments prepared to resume negotiations on a legally binding global plastics treaty in Geneva. The associated Lancet health-policy paper framed plastics as a growing human and planetary health risk, including large economic losses and exposure across the plastic life cycle.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the health warning is tied to an unresolved governance problem. The Geneva treaty session that opened after regional consultations on 2025-08-04 adjourned on 2025-08-15 without consensus on treaty text, with disagreement over issues including production, chemicals of concern, finance, and binding controls. UNEP recorded the process as continuing, while official treaty pages later listed a further resumed session in February 2026.

### Direct impacts
The immediate impact was to strengthen the evidence base available to negotiators by putting health harms, not only waste management and marine pollution, at the center of the plastics debate.

### Indirect impacts
The broader effect was to sharpen a negotiating divide. A health-and-life-cycle framing supports controls on production, design, and toxic chemicals, while several states and industry-aligned positions favored narrower waste-management approaches. The failure to reach consensus left that divide unresolved.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage could connect the Lancet project to the upcoming talks, but it could not yet show that the session would fail to produce a treaty text or that future negotiations would still be burdened by the same scope and implementation disputes.

### One-year assessment
The event was consequential because it marked the moment when plastics policy was explicitly framed as a tracked global-health problem just as treaty diplomacy stalled. Its one-year significance lies in the mismatch between increasingly organized evidence on harm and the inability of the multilateral process to convert that evidence into binding rules.

## 4. The Yemen migrant-boat disaster becomes an Eastern Route warning

**Original date:** 2025-08-04
**Type:** Migration / Maritime Safety / Humanitarian Protection
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-08-04, authorities and the International Organization for Migration reported a deadly migrant-boat capsize off Yemen's Abyan coast. Early counts varied as bodies were recovered, but reports described dozens dead, many missing, and a vessel carrying mostly Ethiopian migrants on the Eastern Route toward Yemen and the Gulf.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the incident reads as part of a worsening route-safety pattern rather than a freak maritime accident. IOM's formal statement said a vessel carrying 200 migrants capsized off Shuqrah on 2025-08-03, with at least 56 dead, 132 missing, and only 12 survivors confirmed at that point. IOM later described five recorded boat incidents over six months causing 378 deaths, and warned that demand for voluntary return and coastal assistance remained high in a constrained humanitarian environment.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was a mass-casualty shipwreck, emergency recovery work along the Abyan coast, and immediate humanitarian needs for survivors and families of the dead and missing.

### Indirect impacts
The broader effect was to reinforce the Eastern Route as a lethal corridor shaped by smuggling networks, poverty, conflict, climate stress, and limited search-and-rescue capacity. Yemen's own war and humanitarian crisis make it both a transit route and a site of additional exposure for migrants after arrival.

### What original coverage missed
Opening coverage captured the death toll and missing-person uncertainty, but it could not yet place the incident inside IOM's later 2025 route-wide accounting, including the acceleration of maritime deaths and the need to activate or expand coastal monitoring and mobile protection support.

### One-year assessment
The disaster was consequential because it made visible the human cost of a migration route that remains active despite extreme risk. Its one-year meaning is a protection failure: deterrence, patrols, and conflict conditions did not remove the incentives to move, while safe pathways and rescue capacity remained insufficient.
