## 1. The Clairton Coke Works explosion becomes a process-safety case

**Original date:** 2025-08-11
**Type:** Industrial Safety / Steelmaking / Process Hazards
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-08-11, an explosion at U.S. Steel's Clairton Coke Works near Pittsburgh killed two workers and sent ten others to hospitals, with five listed in critical but stable condition. Early public information placed the blast around the plant's coke oven batteries 13 and 14, at the largest coking facility in North America.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the event is no longer only an emergency-response story. The Chemical Safety Board reported that the explosion occurred during maintenance on a gas isolation valve and involved a release and ignition of coke oven gas. The board issued interim recommendations on facility siting and worker protection, while OSHA cited U.S. Steel and contractor MPW Industrial Services for serious safety violations tied to flammable-gas work, energy control, explosion hazards, and high-pressure water-system hazards.

### Direct impacts
The direct impacts were two deaths, multiple injuries, structural damage, a prolonged search-and-rescue operation, and federal investigations by CSB and OSHA. OSHA proposed $118,214 in penalties against U.S. Steel and $61,473 against MPW, while the CSB kept recommendations open pending company response or evaluation.

### Indirect impacts
The broader effect was to turn a single explosion into a test of process-safety management at an aging, high-hazard steelmaking facility. The CSB's focus on occupied buildings near hazardous processes widened the case from the failed valve and maintenance procedure to the location of workers during credible explosion, fire, and toxic-release scenarios.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage could describe casualties and the blast location, but it could not yet identify the maintenance sequence, the old gas isolation valve, the facility-siting concern, or the later regulatory findings against both the plant operator and a contractor.

### One-year assessment
The Clairton explosion was consequential because it exposed process-safety risks that remained analytically alive after the emergency ended. One year later, its durable significance lies in whether U.S. Steel and regulators convert the investigation record into safer siting, maintenance, and contractor-control practices at coke-oven facilities.

## 2. New World screwworm preparedness becomes a domestic biosecurity buildout

**Original date:** 2025-08-11
**Type:** Agricultural Biosecurity / Public Health / Livestock
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-08-11, U.S. coverage warned that New World screwworm, a parasitic fly whose larvae infest living tissue, was moving northward and could threaten Texas ranching and public health preparedness. The immediate U.S. story centered on prevention tools, including Texas's synthetic lure program and USDA planning.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the warning has become a live biosecurity program. CDC issued a January 2026 health advisory after animal cases reached Tamaulipas, Mexico, and USDA shifted sterile-fly dispersal toward the U.S.-Mexico border. By June 2026, APHIS confirmed New World screwworm in calves in Zavala County, Texas, while reporting no locally acquired human infestations. USDA also completed a Texas dispersal facility, broke ground on a Texas production facility, and listed operational or expanding sterile-fly capacity in Panama, Mexico, and Texas.

### Direct impacts
The direct impacts included border surveillance, movement controls, public-health alerts, clinician guidance, livestock inspections, confirmed U.S. animal detections, and accelerated sterile-insect infrastructure. The U.S. response moved from preparedness planning into active containment after the Texas detections.

### Indirect impacts
The broader effect was to show how an eradicated livestock pest can re-enter domestic risk planning through regional movement, surveillance gaps, and dependence on specialized biological-control capacity. It connected animal health, human health, trade, border operations, and ranch economics.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage could warn about a possible U.S. invasion, but it could not yet show the January border-facing health advisory, the June Texas calf detections, or the scale of federal investment in sterile-fly dispersal and production.

### One-year assessment
The August 2025 screwworm warning was consequential because it captured a transition from distant outbreak to domestic readiness problem. One year later, the most important result is institutional: the United States is rebuilding biological-control capacity for a pest it had treated as eliminated for decades.

## 3. The Iran-IAEA visit becomes a safeguards-access breakdown

**Original date:** 2025-08-11
**Type:** Nuclear Safeguards / Nonproliferation / Middle East
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-08-11, Iran said a senior International Atomic Energy Agency official would visit Tehran for talks but that no nuclear-site inspections were planned. The visit followed Iran's suspension of cooperation with the agency after the June 2025 U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iranian nuclear sites.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, that no-inspection visit reads as an early point in a broader safeguards breakdown. Iran and the IAEA reached a framework in Cairo in September 2025, but later reporting and statements described continued restrictions, reimposed U.N. measures after snapback, and a near-total loss of IAEA access beyond Bushehr. In June 2026, the IAEA director general told the Board of Governors that the agency had lost continuity of knowledge over previously declared nuclear material at facilities affected by the June 2025 attacks, including highly enriched uranium.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was the failure to restore inspections at the sites most relevant to nonproliferation verification. The IAEA could not verify key information about enriched uranium stocks, centrifuge inventories, and the status of damaged or declared facilities, while Iran made access contingent on new political and legal terms.

### Indirect impacts
The broader effect was to weaken confidence in the safeguards regime during a period when military strikes, sanctions, and diplomacy were all interacting. The dispute shifted attention from whether a negotiated framework could be announced to whether inspectors could actually regain timely, credible access.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage could identify Iran's refusal to allow immediate inspections, but it could not yet show that the September framework would not restore durable access, that snapback would become part of the diplomatic environment, or that the agency would still be warning in June 2026 about lost continuity of knowledge.

### One-year assessment
The August 2025 IAEA visit was consequential because it marked diplomacy without verification. One year later, the durable issue is not the meeting itself, but the precedent of extended inspection loss after military attacks on safeguarded facilities.

## 4. CATL's lithium mine halt becomes a battery-minerals supply-discipline test

**Original date:** 2025-08-11
**Type:** Critical Minerals / Electric Vehicles / Battery Supply Chains
**Confidence:** Medium

### Then
On 2025-08-11, CATL said it had suspended mining at its Jianxiawo lithium project in Yichun after a mining license expired. The news immediately moved lithium markets: futures and lithium-miner shares rose because traders read the shutdown as possible supply discipline in a market hit by oversupply and weak prices.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the mine halt looks less like a brief administrative pause than a nearly year-long supply and compliance episode. CATL had to work through renewed approvals, market participants repeatedly watched for a restart, and in late June 2026 the company secured the safety-production permit viewed as the final key hurdle. Reuters reported in July 2026 that the mine had obtained the permit needed to restart after the long suspension.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was to remove a sizeable source of lithium carbonate equivalent from the market for months and temporarily support lithium prices and mining equities. The mine's capacity was widely reported at about 46,000 metric tons of lithium carbonate equivalent a year, roughly 3 percent of forecast 2025 global output.

### Indirect impacts
The broader effect was to show that battery-mineral oversupply can be managed not only by corporate production cuts but also by permitting, royalty, safety, and land-use decisions. That matters for automakers and battery manufacturers because upstream mineral availability can change through regulatory process as much as through geology.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage captured the price reaction and the possibility of a three-month halt, but it could not yet show the restart delays, the long approval sequence, or the way the case would become a signal for China's wider handling of lithium-sector overcapacity.

### One-year assessment
CATL's August 2025 mine halt was consequential because it made one Chinese permit decision visible across the global EV supply chain. One year later, the episode reads as a battery-minerals supply-discipline test: temporary in form, but important as evidence that critical-mineral markets can be moved by administrative controls as well as demand.
