## 1. EPA's endangerment proposal becomes a Clean Air Act rollback

**Original date:** 2025-07-29
**Type:** Climate Regulation / Administrative Law / United States
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On July 29, 2025, EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin announced a proposal to rescind the 2009 greenhouse-gas endangerment finding and to remove greenhouse-gas standards for light-, medium-, and heavy-duty highway vehicles and engines. The agency described the finding as the legal prerequisite for vehicle greenhouse-gas rules under Clean Air Act section 202.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the proposal is no longer only a deregulatory signal. EPA finalized the rescission on February 12, 2026, and its rule page says the action also repealed later greenhouse-gas emission standards for covered on-highway vehicles and engines. The rule is therefore a live administrative-law and climate-policy marker rather than an unrealized proposal.

### Direct impacts
The direct effect was to remove EPA's asserted Clean Air Act basis for greenhouse-gas standards for new motor vehicles and engines. EPA said manufacturers would no longer have future obligations for measuring, controlling, or reporting greenhouse-gas emissions under the repealed highway-vehicle rules, while criteria-pollutant and air-toxic standards were left outside this action.

### Indirect impacts
The action shifted climate regulation from rule implementation to litigation and statutory authority. It also changed planning assumptions for automakers, states, environmental groups, and future administrations by testing how far an agency can reverse a scientific and legal predicate that had supported climate regulation since 2009.

### What original coverage missed
Immediate coverage could identify the proposal as an attack on the foundation of federal climate rules, but it could not know whether the agency would finalize it. The one-year record shows that the proposal became operative agency policy within seven months, making the durability of the reversal the central unresolved question.

### One-year assessment
The July 29 announcement mattered because it was the formal step that moved a long-running conservative legal argument into binding federal action. Its lasting significance depends on judicial review, but after one year it stands as one of the clearest examples of climate policy being remade through administrative-law interpretation.

## 2. The AU rejection of Sudan's parallel government becomes a fragmentation marker

**Original date:** 2025-07-29
**Type:** Conflict Governance / State Fragmentation / Sudan
**Confidence:** Medium

### Then
On July 29, 2025, the African Union Peace and Security Council condemned the RSF-led Sudan Founding Alliance's announcement of a parallel government and urged states and international actors not to recognize it. The statement followed the July 26 announcement from Nyala that named RSF leader Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo as head of a presidential council and Mohamed Hassan al-Taishi as prime minister.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the AU's warning has aged into a wider record of institutional division. A July 2026 UK country-policy note says both the army-aligned authorities in Port Sudan and the RSF in Nyala claimed civilian governments, and that the RSF had established state institutions including a parallel central bank and currency. Human Rights Watch's 2026 world report also recorded the July 2025 creation of an RSF-aligned parallel government in Darfur.

### Direct impacts
The AU statement set a regional non-recognition position and framed the RSF-aligned structure as a threat to Sudan's sovereignty and territorial integrity. It gave member states a clear diplomatic line while leaving the armed balance on the ground unchanged.

### Indirect impacts
The move hardened the risk that Sudan's war would become not only a contest for territory but a contest between administrative systems. Parallel institutions can complicate humanitarian access, financial control, local taxation, documentation, and any future negotiation over a unified transition.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage focused on whether the new authority would be recognized. The one-year view shows that formal recognition was not the only issue: even without broad recognition, parallel institutions could still become facts of administration in areas under RSF control.

### One-year assessment
The July 29 AU response mattered because it marked the external boundary around Sudan's fragmentation. It did not prevent the growth of rival administrative structures, but it preserved a formal continental position against legitimizing partition by force.

## 3. Angola's fuel protests turn subsidy reform into a social-stability test

**Original date:** 2025-07-29
**Type:** Fiscal Policy / Protest / Angola
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On July 29, 2025, Angolan police said four people had been killed and more than 500 arrested during protests in Luanda sparked by a diesel price increase. The rise followed the government's effort to cut fuel subsidies and quickly affected minibus taxi fares, a central form of transport for many residents.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the protests sit inside a longer fiscal-adjustment record. The IMF reported in September 2025 that completion of fuel-subsidy reform had been delayed to 2028 amid social tension. In its 2026 Article IV materials, the IMF said Angola's 2025 subsidy savings fell short of projections and that subsidy reform remained a priority, but one that needed stronger social protection and communication.

### Direct impacts
The immediate impacts were deaths, mass arrests, transport disruption, property damage, and a visible political backlash against a price change that moved quickly from the fuel market into household transport costs.

### Indirect impacts
The unrest weakened the assumption that subsidy removal could proceed as a mostly technical fiscal measure. It also exposed the political cost of reform in an oil-producing country where many citizens still face high living costs and limited confidence that public savings will be converted into broad social benefit.

### What original coverage missed
Early reports captured the violence and arrests but could not fully show how the episode would alter the reform timetable. The later IMF record indicates that social acceptance and mitigation design became central constraints on the pace of fiscal consolidation.

### One-year assessment
The July 29 unrest mattered because it made subsidy reform an explicit governance test. Angola still faces pressure to reduce fuel-subsidy costs, but the one-year record shows that implementation capacity, public trust, and protection for vulnerable users are as important as the budget arithmetic.

## 4. NOAA's satellite-data reprieve becomes a weather-infrastructure warning

**Original date:** 2025-07-29
**Type:** Weather Infrastructure / Disaster Preparedness / United States
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On July 29, 2025, NOAA said there would be no interruption in Defense Meteorological Satellite Program data used by forecasters after the Navy's Fleet Numerical Meteorology and Oceanography Center reversed plans to stop distribution at the end of July. The data included microwave observations important for seeing storm structure through clouds and darkness.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the practical outage was avoided, but the dependence remains temporary. NASA's Global Precipitation Measurement mission and the National Snow and Ice Data Center said SSMIS data processing would continue until the sensor fails or the DMSP program formally ends, then expected in September 2026. NSIDC also warned users to evaluate alternatives because gaps and sensor failures remained possible.

### Direct impacts
The direct result was continuity for hurricane, precipitation, sea-ice, and other environmental data users who had been preparing for a sudden cutoff. It preserved a data stream used in operational forecasting and research through at least the remaining life of the aging mission.

### Indirect impacts
The episode showed how civilian disaster preparedness can depend on defense-owned infrastructure, interagency data sharing, and aging systems with limited replacement margin. It also made weather-data continuity a governance issue, not just a technical one.

### What original coverage missed
Immediate coverage understandably framed the decision as a crisis averted. The one-year view adds a more restrained conclusion: the reprieve bought time, but did not itself solve the transition from DMSP to replacement microwave data systems.

### One-year assessment
The July 29 reversal mattered because it prevented an avoidable forecasting-data shock during hurricane season. Its lasting significance is as a warning that weather resilience depends on boring but fragile data pipelines whose governance can affect public safety.
