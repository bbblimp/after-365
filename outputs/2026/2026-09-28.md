# After 365 - 2026-09-28

## Daily Summary

The 2025-09-28 lookback produced an intentionally compact three-entry report. The final set follows three distinct institutional consequences: youth protests over basic services in Madagascar opened the way to a military takeover rather than accountable civilian reform; Swiss voters approved a housing-tax overhaul whose implementation was later fixed for 2029; and Eric Adams's withdrawal clarified a New York mayoral contest that produced unusually high turnout and a change in political direction. Moldova's election, Iran sanctions, Typhoon Bualoi, Gaza diplomacy, and Denmark's drone response were consequential but excluded because they repeated subjects selected during the previous seven run days; the Seychelles runoff, Swiss e-ID vote, Michigan church attack, U.S. shutdown standoff, and other candidates were also considered but offered less additional analytical value or a weaker one-year record.

## 1. Madagascar's service protests become a warning about military-led transition

**Original date:** 2025-09-28
**Type:** Political Transition / Civil-Military Relations / Madagascar
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-09-28, Madagascar remained under curfew as youth-led demonstrations over chronic electricity and water failures, corruption, and freedom of expression continued in Antananarivo and other cities. President Andry Rajoelina had returned to the country after his absence fueled speculation, but a planned press conference was canceled and Gen Z Madagascar called for further mobilization. Police had used tear gas, people had been killed or injured during the first days of unrest, and looting and shortages complicated the political protest.

### State after 365 days
One year later, the protest wave is best understood as the opening of a regime-change crisis rather than a successful public-service reform movement. Rajoelina dismissed his government on September 29 but was forced from power in October after part of the armed forces defected. Colonel Michael Randrianirina took power, the African Union suspended Madagascar, and the new leadership promised elections within two years. By 2026, rights groups and anniversary reporting described protesters as frustrated that military rule had reproduced exclusion and repression while water, electricity, and accountability demands remained unresolved.

### Direct impacts
The immediate crisis brought deaths and injuries, curfews, commercial disruption, government dismissal, Rajoelina's flight, parliamentary impeachment, and a military takeover. Madagascar's suspension from the African Union added a diplomatic cost, while the transitional authorities set an election horizon extending to 2027.

### Indirect impacts
The protests altered the country's constitutional order without securing a civilian transfer of power. They also showed how a movement grounded in daily service failures can create an opening for armed actors: the military could present itself as aligned with public anger, then control the transition and its timetable.

### What original coverage missed
Coverage on September 28 captured an escalating protest movement and a president struggling to regain control, but it could not yet show how quickly the armed forces would become the decisive institution. The subsequent year also complicated the assumption that removing the incumbent would translate into greater youth participation or better basic services.

### One-year assessment
The Madagascar protests mattered because they toppled a government, but their one-year record is cautionary. The episode did not produce a settled democratic opening; it replaced an unpopular elected administration with a military-led transition whose accountability and election promises remain contested. This entry is included despite a recent Nepal protest item because Madagascar's defining consequence was a completed military seizure of power.

## 2. The Swiss housing-tax vote becomes a long-lead federalism test

**Original date:** 2025-09-28
**Type:** Tax Policy / Housing / Direct Democracy
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-09-28, Swiss voters and cantons approved a constitutional amendment allowing cantonal property taxes on predominantly owner-used second homes. Because Parliament had legally linked that amendment to a wider reform, the 57.7% vote also approved abolition of the federal and cantonal taxation of imputed rental value for owner-occupied main and second homes, together with tighter limits on mortgage-interest and maintenance deductions.

### State after 365 days
One year later, the vote has produced a defined but deliberately slow implementation path rather than an immediate tax change. In April 2026, the Federal Council set 2029-01-01 as the start date, giving cantons time to rewrite their tax laws and decide whether to impose the new second-home property tax. The distributional effect remains conditional: at low mortgage rates many low-debt owners are expected to pay less, while at high rates the loss of interest deductions can raise liabilities for more heavily indebted owners.

### Direct impacts
The ballot settled the reform's legal authority and triggered federal and cantonal implementation work. From 2029, owner-occupiers will no longer declare an imputed rent as income, most associated deductions will be restricted, and cantons may use a special second-home levy to offset lost revenue.

### Indirect impacts
The reform shifts housing incentives and fiscal risk across borrowers, retirees, tourism cantons, municipalities, and the federal government. It may reduce incentives to retain mortgage debt for tax purposes, but the ultimate revenue and household effects will depend on interest rates and on twenty-six cantonal implementation choices.

### What original coverage missed
The referendum result looked like a single national decision, but implementation exposed its federal character. The long lead time and optional cantonal second-home tax mean that much of the policy's real incidence will be determined after the vote, through technical rules and subnational legislation.

### One-year assessment
The September 2025 vote mattered because it ended a longstanding principle of Swiss owner-occupied housing taxation, but it did not deliver an immediate windfall. Its one-year significance lies in converting a popular vote into a multi-year coordination task whose winners, losers, and revenue effects remain sensitive to mortgage rates and cantonal choices.

## 3. Eric Adams's exit becomes a New York political-transition marker

**Original date:** 2025-09-28
**Type:** Urban Governance / Electoral Politics / New York City
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-09-28, New York City Mayor Eric Adams ended his independent campaign for a second term, five weeks before the general election. He said the aftermath of his dismissed federal corruption case and the withholding of public matching funds had made a viable campaign impossible. Polling had placed him in single digits, and his departure left Democratic nominee Zohran Mamdani, former governor Andrew Cuomo, and Republican Curtis Sliwa as the principal active candidates, although Adams's name remained on the printed ballot.

### State after 365 days
One year later, Adams's withdrawal is best seen as the close of a one-term administration and a marker in a broader political realignment, not as the single cause of the result. Mamdani won the November election and took office on 2026-01-01. The 2025 general election drew 2.2 million voters, a 41.6% turnout and the city's highest mayoral-election participation in more than fifty years; participation among voters aged 18 to 29 more than tripled from 2021. Mamdani's first months moved the campaign's affordability, housing, labor, and immigrant-protection arguments into city governance.

### Direct impacts
Adams stopped active campaigning and donors and voters could treat the race as a contest among the remaining candidates, though his ballot line persisted. Mamdani's subsequent victory produced a mayoral transition on January 1, 2026, while Adams left office after one term.

### Indirect impacts
The withdrawal underscored how legal controversy, public-finance rules, and low approval can disable an incumbent even after criminal charges are dismissed. The election also mobilized younger and newly registered voters at unusual rates and gave a democratic-socialist administration control of the country's largest municipal government.

### What original coverage missed
Immediate coverage focused on whether Adams's departure would consolidate anti-Mamdani votes behind Cuomo. It could not establish how voters would redistribute, and later turnout data cautions against reducing the result to elite candidate coordination: the electorate itself expanded sharply, especially among young and newly registered voters.

### One-year assessment
Adams's September 28 exit mattered less as a tactical endorsement—he made none—than as formal recognition that his coalition and campaign had collapsed. The durable consequence was a clean opening for a new governing era, validated by unusually broad participation, although Mamdani's record remains too young for a settled judgment on policy outcomes.
