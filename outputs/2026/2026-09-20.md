## 1. The Collins airport cyberattack becomes an aviation supply-chain resilience test

**Original date:** 2025-09-20
**Type:** Cybersecurity / Aviation Infrastructure / Supply-Chain Risk
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-09-20, a cyberattack disrupted check-in and boarding systems at major European airports, including Heathrow, Brussels, and Berlin Brandenburg. Airports said the problem centered on a third-party provider rather than the airports or airlines themselves. Collins Aerospace, owned by RTX, identified a cyber-related disruption affecting its MUSE passenger-processing software, forcing manual check-in and baggage procedures.

### State after 365 days
One year later, the incident reads as a supply-chain resilience case rather than a simple airport outage. RTX later disclosed to the SEC that it had become aware on 2025-09-19 of a ransomware incident involving systems supporting MUSE, and Reuters reported continuing recovery work and a British arrest in the investigation. The financial impact was not described by RTX as material, but the operational impact exposed how a shared vendor platform could affect multiple hubs at once.

### Direct impacts
The direct effects were passenger delays, flight cancellations, manual check-in and baggage processing, and emergency work by airports, airlines, Collins, cybersecurity agencies, and law enforcement. Brussels faced the most persistent disruption, while Heathrow and Berlin also operated under workarounds.

### Indirect impacts
The broader effect was to move aviation cyber risk from a back-office concern into a visible critical-infrastructure problem. The incident strengthened the argument that resilience planning must include common-use airport systems and vendor platforms, not only airline networks or air-traffic-control systems.

### What original coverage missed
Initial reporting could identify disrupted airports and the Collins platform, but it could not yet confirm the ransomware characterization, the SEC disclosure, the length of recovery at affected airports, or the law-enforcement follow-up.

### One-year assessment
The Collins incident was consequential because it showed how ransomware against a specialized aviation supplier can produce cross-border operational effects even when flight safety systems are not the target. Its legacy is less a single outage than a practical warning about dependency concentration in airport passenger processing.

## 2. The H-1B fee confusion becomes a skilled-labor policy fight

**Original date:** 2025-09-20
**Type:** Immigration Policy / Skilled Labor / Administrative Law
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-09-20, the White House moved to clarify confusion from President Donald Trump's new H-1B proclamation, saying the $100,000 charge would apply to new petitions and not to existing visa holders re-entering the United States. The clarification followed immediate anxiety among companies and workers after the prior day's proclamation and public statements suggested a much broader fee.

### State after 365 days
A year later, the September 20 clarification has become part of a broader fight over executive authority, skilled immigration, and fee design. The 2025 proclamation took effect on 2025-09-21; by mid-2026, litigation had produced a Massachusetts district-court order vacating implementing actions, an appeal, and a First Circuit stay fight. In September 2026, the White House issued a new proclamation extending the policy for another year, while DHS separately proposed a new H-1B fee through rulemaking.

### Direct impacts
The immediate impact was practical uncertainty for employers, universities, hospitals, visa holders, and prospective applicants. Companies had to distinguish between existing visa holders, workers outside the United States, new petitions, and possible national-interest exceptions.

### Indirect impacts
The policy pushed skilled immigration deeper into administrative-law and labor-market politics. It also encouraged employers to reassess foreign hiring, offshore capacity, university staffing, and contingency planning around petition timing.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage captured the panic and White House clarification, but it could not yet show the later litigation path, the proposed DHS fee rule, or the administration's decision to try extending the proclamation despite court challenges.

### One-year assessment
The September 20 clarification mattered because implementation details became the policy's real terrain. What first looked like a blunt price shock became a test of whether a president could reshape a statutory skilled-worker program through entry restrictions, agency guidance, and later rulemaking.

## 3. The Hague anti-immigration riots become a political-violence prosecution record

**Original date:** 2025-09-20
**Type:** Democratic Institutions / Far-Right Violence / Public Order
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-09-20, an anti-immigration demonstration in The Hague turned violent weeks before the Netherlands' general election. Rioters clashed with police, set a police vehicle on fire, blocked a highway, attacked journalists and police, and vandalized the D66 party office. Dutch political leaders condemned the violence and treated the attack on a party office as an assault on democratic politics.

### State after 365 days
One year later, the riot has become a prosecution and public-order record. Dutch courts issued a sequence of convictions in 2026 for violence around the Malieveld demonstration, including prison and suspended sentences. By mid-2026, court notices described dozens of convictions linked to the unrest; another conviction followed on 2026-09-18. A new far-right protest in The Hague in September 2026 also drew official warnings and policing concerns, underscoring the event's persistence as a political-violence reference point.

### Direct impacts
The immediate impacts included injuries and threats to police and others, damage to public and private property, attacks on a political-party office, arrests, and parliamentary debate over whether the violence should be treated as right-extremist.

### Indirect impacts
The riot hardened Dutch debate over protest boundaries, migration politics, and protection of democratic institutions. It also gave prosecutors and courts a concrete test of how to handle group violence that mixed anti-immigration mobilization, extremist symbols, and attacks on political targets.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage could report the disorder, arrests, and condemnations, but it could not yet show the later scale of prosecutions or how the 2025 violence would be invoked when policing similar demonstrations a year later.

### One-year assessment
The Hague riot was consequential because it moved beyond ordinary election-season protest into violence against police, journalists, and a party office. The one-year record shows that Dutch institutions treated it as a criminal and democratic-order problem, not only as public disorder.

## 4. The Vatican appeal becomes a due-process test for financial reform

**Original date:** 2025-09-20
**Type:** Religious Institutions / Financial Governance / Judicial Procedure
**Confidence:** Medium

### Then
On 2025-09-20, reporting previewed the appeals phase of the Vatican's major financial trial over a London property investment, after thousands of pages of WhatsApp messages raised questions about witness handling, prosecutorial conduct, and Pope Francis' direct role in the investigation. The appeal followed convictions including that of Cardinal Angelo Becciu.

### State after 365 days
One year later, the appeal had become a procedural legitimacy test for Vatican justice. In March 2026, the Vatican appeals court ordered partial renewal of parts of the proceedings, identifying procedural shortcomings and requiring renewed evidentiary steps, while leaving parts of the first-instance record intact. The case therefore shifted from whether financial misconduct occurred to whether the Vatican's own reformed legal system could sustain high-profile convictions under appellate scrutiny.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was to reopen contested evidentiary questions and require additional procedural steps at the appellate level. The order prolonged the case and kept the Becciu conviction and related financial-reform narrative under review.

### Indirect impacts
The appeal affected the credibility of Pope Francis-era financial reforms and raised broader questions about separation of powers, prosecutorial disclosure, and the Vatican's ability to produce judgments accepted outside its own legal system.

### What original coverage missed
The September 20 preview could identify the explosive text messages and likely defense strategy, but it could not yet show that the appeals court would find enough procedural defects to require partial renewal of the proceedings.

### One-year assessment
The appeal was consequential because it converted a symbolic anti-corruption trial into a test of institutional due process. The case still matters less for the London building itself than for whether Vatican financial accountability can be paired with credible legal procedure.
