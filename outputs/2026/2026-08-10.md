## 1. The Nvidia-AMD fee becomes export-control monetization

**Original date:** 2025-08-10
**Type:** Semiconductor Export Controls / Artificial Intelligence / U.S.-China Technology
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-08-10, reporting said Nvidia and AMD had agreed to give the U.S. government 15 percent of revenue from certain AI-chip sales to China in exchange for export licenses. The immediate story was unusual because it did not simply tighten or loosen export controls; it attached a direct fiscal return to permission to sell sensitive semiconductors.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the arrangement looks less like a settled export-control model than a precedent-setting improvisation. Nvidia later told investors that U.S. officials expected the government to receive at least 15 percent of revenue from licensed sales, while also noting that no regulation had codified the requirement. The company reported only limited licensed H20 revenue under the August 2025 approvals and said a February 2026 H200 license covered small amounts for specific China-based customers, with uncertainty about Chinese import permission.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was to reopen a controlled channel for some Nvidia H20 and AMD MI308 sales to China while creating a government revenue expectation tied to export licenses. It turned an export-control decision into a bargain involving market access, national-security claims, corporate revenue, and public receipts.

### Indirect impacts
The broader effect was to blur the line between security regulation and industrial bargaining. The episode gave critics a concrete example of export controls being softened for payment, while giving firms a reminder that access to China could be turned on, paused, or repriced through licensing discretion.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage could identify the novelty and legal unease of the 15 percent fee, but it could not yet show how limited the actual licensed sales would be or that the requirement would remain an expectation rather than a clearly codified rule.

### One-year assessment
The August 2025 chip-fee arrangement was consequential because it made export control more transactional. One year later, its durability remains uncertain, but the precedent matters: strategic-technology licensing became a venue for government revenue negotiation as well as security screening.

## 2. The UCLA demand becomes a federal research-funding coercion test

**Original date:** 2025-08-10
**Type:** Higher Education / Civil Rights Enforcement / Federal Research Funding
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-08-10, the University of California was publicly reviewing a Trump administration settlement proposal for UCLA after the government froze hundreds of millions of dollars in federal funding. The dispute was framed around antisemitism and campus protests, but the scale of the requested payment made it a wider test of public-university governance and research funding.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the issue has moved into litigation rather than a completed settlement. UC said the proposed payment would devastate the public university system, and the released proposal later showed an even larger $1.2 billion demand tied to policy changes. A higher-education policy tracker recorded that a federal judge blocked the settlement proposal in late 2025, the administration dropped an appeal in February 2026, and the Justice Department then pursued separate UCLA-related civil-rights lawsuits in 2026.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was uncertainty for UCLA researchers, students, and administrators while federal research grants were frozen or threatened. The dispute forced UC leadership and regents into emergency deliberations over whether to negotiate, litigate, or absorb severe financial and governance conditions.

### Indirect impacts
The broader effect was to make federal research funding a central instrument in the national fight over campus protest, antisemitism enforcement, DEI policy, and institutional autonomy. Other universities could read the UCLA demand as a warning about the scale of leverage attached to grant funding.

### What original coverage missed
Immediate coverage could see the size of the demand and the political confrontation, but it could not yet show that the proposal would be blocked in court and followed by separate civil-rights litigation rather than resolved as a single settlement.

### One-year assessment
The UCLA demand was consequential because it tested whether civil-rights enforcement could be paired with sweeping financial and governance pressure on a public university. One year later, the durable issue is not only UCLA's campus response, but the legal boundary around using federal grants to force institutional change.

## 3. The South Sudan-Uganda border probe becomes a recurring-displacement warning

**Original date:** 2025-08-10
**Type:** Border Security / Regional Stability / Civilian Displacement
**Confidence:** Medium

### Then
On 2025-08-10, South Sudan and Uganda said they would investigate deadly clashes along their shared border after fighting between their forces displaced civilians in Central Equatoria's Kajo-Keji area. The announcement mattered because Uganda was not a distant adversary: it was also a key security backer of President Salva Kiir's government.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the border issue has not disappeared. Humanitarian reporting in late 2025 recorded more than 16,000 people displaced by the tensions, and local authorities said many returned only as security improved. In July 2026, local and Ugandan military reports described renewed clashes and a reconciliation meeting that adopted resolutions on re-demarcating a disputed 6.12-kilometre stretch, improving intelligence coordination, and managing cross-border incidents.

### Direct impacts
The direct impact was deaths among security forces, local displacement, and the creation of a joint investigation process. Communities around Kajo-Keji and Uganda's West Nile border area faced disrupted schools, markets, health access, and basic safety.

### Indirect impacts
The broader effect was to expose a weakness in an alliance that South Sudan also relies on for internal security. A border dispute between nominal partners complicated trade, local coexistence, and the credibility of regional security arrangements.

### What original coverage missed
The initial announcement suggested a contained investigation. It could not yet show how displacement would persist, how civilians would bear the main burden, or that the same border area would see renewed armed confrontation within the following year.

### One-year assessment
The August 2025 probe was consequential because it revealed that unresolved border demarcation can destabilize even close security relationships. One year later, the case is best read as a recurring-displacement warning: dialogue reduced escalation at moments, but it did not remove the underlying territorial dispute.

## 4. The Sindirgi earthquake becomes a reconstruction and fault-science case

**Original date:** 2025-08-10
**Type:** Earthquake Risk / Reconstruction / Seismology
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-08-10, a magnitude-6.1 earthquake struck near Sindirgi in Turkey's Balikesir province, killing one person, injuring dozens, collapsing or damaging buildings, and producing aftershocks felt across parts of western Turkey. Early official reporting treated it as a damaging but limited event rather than a national catastrophe.

### State after 365 days
After 365 days, the event has become both a reconstruction project and a scientific case study. Turkish reporting in June 2026 said construction was nearing completion on 1,180 independent units, including 736 disaster homes, 385 village houses, businesses, and barns, for people affected by the August and October 2025 Sindirgi earthquakes. New research in 2026 examined the August rupture, aftershock migration, and the difficulty of identifying the source fault in a complex western Anatolia fault intersection.

### Direct impacts
The direct impacts were fatalities and injuries, emergency inspections, damage assessments, temporary displacement, and subsequent housing construction. The October 2025 earthquake in the same district broadened the reconstruction obligation beyond the first August event.

### Indirect impacts
The broader effect was to make Sindirgi a practical test of post-earthquake housing delivery and a scientific marker for western Anatolia seismic risk. The sequence raised questions about how moderate earthquakes interact with mapped faults, unmapped structures, and local building vulnerability.

### What original coverage missed
Initial coverage could document casualties and visible damage, but it could not yet show the later October companion event, the scale of the housing program, or the 2026 research debate over source mechanics and aftershock behavior.

### One-year assessment
The August 2025 earthquake was consequential because it converted a moderate regional quake into a durable reconstruction and risk-mapping case. Its legacy is not only the immediate damage, but the evidence it added about fault complexity and the administrative demand for rapid, local rebuilding.
