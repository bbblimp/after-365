## 1. The Cebu earthquake becomes a fault-mapping and long-recovery case

**Original date:** 2025-09-30
**Type:** Disaster Risk / Seismology / Recovery
**Confidence:** High

### Then
At 9:59 p.m. Philippine time on 2025-09-30, a shallow magnitude-6.9 earthquake struck offshore northern Cebu near Bogo City. Severe shaking collapsed or damaged homes and public buildings, cut power, triggered landslides and a short-lived warning for minor sea-level disturbance, and began a long aftershock sequence. Early casualty figures rose rapidly as authorities reached damaged communities.

### State after 365 days
The established record is larger and more instructive than the first reports. Philippine authorities ultimately reported 79 deaths and hundreds of injuries, while PHIVOLCS fieldwork attributed the event to the newly identified Bogo Bay Fault. Government disaster reporting was still being updated on 2026-09-17, close to the anniversary, indicating that relief and recovery remained an active administrative project rather than a closed emergency.

### Direct impacts
An October 17 official tally recorded 79 deaths, 559 injuries, 747,979 affected people, 126,934 damaged houses, and 7,295 destroyed houses. Power in the affected electric cooperative's service area was restored by October 5, but damaged homes, roads, churches, schools, hospitals, and municipal buildings required longer assessment and repair. Cebu launched an early-recovery plan, while social-welfare and livelihood programs continued beyond the immediate response.

### Indirect impacts
The earthquake changed the hazard map by identifying the Bogo Bay Fault and strengthened the case for fault-aware land-use decisions, inspections, and resilient construction in northern Cebu. Recovery was then complicated by Typhoon Kalmaegi in November 2025, which hit communities still dealing with earthquake displacement and structural damage, showing how sequential hazards can overwhelm otherwise rapid service restoration.

### What original coverage missed
The first-night reports could not know the final human and housing toll or identify the causative fault. They also could not anticipate that power would return quickly while household and public-infrastructure recovery would continue for nearly a year, or that another major disaster would strike the same province during that recovery.

### One-year assessment
The earthquake was not only a brief rescue story. It became a lasting disaster-governance case: a newly mapped fault, a much larger housing burden than early infrastructure counts suggested, and an official assistance record still open near the anniversary. Fast restoration of electricity was real, but it should not be mistaken for complete recovery.

## 2. Haiti's new gang force becomes an implementation-gap test

**Original date:** 2025-09-30
**Type:** International Security / Mission Design / Haiti
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-09-30, the UN Security Council adopted Resolution 2793 by 12 votes to none, with China, Pakistan, and Russia abstaining. It authorized states to replace the Kenya-led Multinational Security Support mission with a Gang Suppression Force of up to 5,500 uniformed personnel and 50 civilians, gave it authority for targeted operations and arrests, and requested a UN Support Office in Haiti to supply logistics and operational support.

### State after 365 days
The resolution produced an operating structure, but not the promised scale within its first year. UNSOH became fully operational by April 2026, the new force began patrols on June 1 and carried out its first targeted operation on June 21. A July UN report counted 1,083 military personnel and 16 civilians, far below the authorized ceiling, even as additional contingents and facilities were being prepared and gang violence continued.

### Direct impacts
The mission acquired a headquarters, medical and evacuation capacity, a joint operations centre, forward-base preparations, and UN-funded logistics. Its first targeted operation in Tabarre resulted in 14 arrests and the destruction of hundreds of Molotov cocktails. By July, $223.5 million had been pledged to the expanded trust fund and $184.2 million received, but deployment remained incomplete and the security situation continued to intensify beyond Port-au-Prince.

### Indirect impacts
Resolution 2793 shifted the central question from whether outside assistance would continue to whether a stronger mandate could be staffed, financed, supervised, and connected to Haitian institutions. It also embedded human-rights due-diligence, child-protection, and national-capacity benchmarks in mission design. Those safeguards matter because security operations themselves accounted for a large share of documented casualties in early 2026.

### What original coverage missed
The vote created expectations around a 5,550-person force, but authorization was not deployment. One year of planning showed that logistics, force generation, financing, legal arrangements, and oversight were substantive parts of the intervention, not background details. Early claims of either rescue or failure were therefore premature.

### One-year assessment
The resolution mattered: it created UNSOH, brought new contingents, and enabled the force's first operations. It had not, however, closed the gap between mandate and capacity by the anniversary. The durable lesson is that a more forceful Security Council design can improve operational foundations without quickly reversing a security crisis when personnel and state capacity lag behind the authorization.

## 3. Kabila's death sentence becomes an opposition and dialogue barrier

**Original date:** 2025-09-30
**Type:** Judicial Accountability / Opposition Politics / Armed Conflict
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-09-30, the High Military Court in Kinshasa convicted former president Joseph Kabila in absentia of treason, war crimes, conspiracy, and organizing an insurrection over alleged support for the Rwanda-backed M23 rebellion. It sentenced him to death, ordered his arrest, and imposed the judgment after a trial in which Kabila was absent and unrepresented. Kabila denied the allegations, and his party called the case political.

### State after 365 days
The sentence remained unenforced one year later, but it did not fade into a merely symbolic verdict. Kabila appeared outside Congo despite the order, the United States sanctioned him in April 2026 for alleged support to M23 and the Congo River Alliance, and Congolese authorities increased pressure on his political camp. His possible place in a proposed national dialogue remained contested precisely because the judgment had turned a former president into both a wanted man and a still-relevant opposition figure.

### Direct impacts
The ruling exposed Kabila to arrest and property measures, while his PPRD party was suspended and remained under threat of dissolution; party officials were also arrested. U.S. sanctions later blocked property under U.S. jurisdiction and generally prohibited U.S. transactions with him. These measures reinforced his isolation without producing his surrender or execution.

### Indirect impacts
The case tied accountability for the eastern rebellion to domestic political competition. It narrowed the space for an inclusive dialogue by making Kabila's participation legally and politically difficult, while criticism of the rushed military trial and absence of a defence raised fair-trial concerns. The verdict therefore functioned simultaneously as a security accusation, a capital sentence, and an instrument reshaping opposition politics.

### What original coverage missed
Immediate reports focused on whether an absent defendant could be arrested and whether the sentence would be carried out. The more durable effect was political: the judgment was followed by party pressure, foreign sanctions, and continuing arguments over whether reconciliation could include Kabila. It neither removed him from political relevance nor resolved the M23 conflict.

### One-year assessment
The sentence did not deliver a clear endpoint. It became a durable constraint on Congolese politics and an unresolved test of whether accountability claims can be pursued through proceedings seen by critics as lacking basic safeguards. External sanctions strengthened the allegations' practical consequences, but the judgment's legitimacy and Kabila's role in any national dialogue remained disputed.

## 4. Sora 2 becomes a short-lived generative-video product experiment

**Original date:** 2025-09-30
**Type:** Artificial Intelligence / Generative Video / Product Strategy
**Confidence:** High

### Then
On 2025-09-30, OpenAI released Sora 2, describing it as a major advance in controllable video and synchronized audio generation, and introduced an invite-based iOS social app built around creation, remixing, a feed, and verified likeness-based 'characters.' The launch also emphasized safeguards including consent controls, visible and invisible provenance signals, and C2PA metadata.

### State after 365 days
The product branch proved much shorter-lived than the launch language suggested. OpenAI states that the Sora product was no longer available as of 2026-04-26. It then deprecated the Sora 2 video-generation API and removed its aliases and snapshots on 2026-09-24, six days before the anniversary. The underlying research significance of generated video did not disappear, but this particular app-and-API offering did.

### Direct impacts
The release briefly gave users a dedicated social environment for generated video, audio, remixing, and consent-based likeness insertion. It also made content provenance and identity control part of the product rather than an external policy discussion. Within seven months the standalone product was withdrawn, and within a year developers also lost the Sora 2 API endpoint.

### Indirect impacts
Sora 2 demonstrated both the rapid improvement and the volatility of frontier-media products. Its system card and launch materials preserved a useful safety baseline around sexual content, likeness misuse, minors, provenance, and feed design, even though the distribution product did not endure. The episode cautions against treating a high-profile model launch as evidence of a stable platform or lasting developer dependency.

### What original coverage missed
Launch-day coverage concentrated on technical capability, the social feed, copyright questions, and misuse risks. It could not anticipate that availability itself would become the clearest one-year result. The short lifecycle means adoption, competitive significance, and social effects should not be inferred from the launch alone.

### One-year assessment
Sora 2 mattered as a compact experiment in pairing generative video with identity-aware social distribution and embedded provenance. As a standalone product and API, however, it faded within the year. The most defensible conclusion is not that generative video failed, but that this specific packaging was not durable enough to support the expansive expectations attached to its debut.
